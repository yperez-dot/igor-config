# AARP Med Supp — chargebacks, recovery, and if UHC kills the schedule

**Still no dollar grid.** This is how they take money back and what happens if the contract ends.

Tone when you say this: **not friendly.** Rule. Consequence. What the team must do. Do not cushion it.

## When they claw back

If a policy is **lapsed, cancelled, not issued, not taken, refunded, or rescinded**, UHC recovers commissions **including advances**.

- Window: **12 months from original effective date**
- **Rapid lapse (in force ≤ 3 months):** **100%** of all commissions paid on that policy
- Chargeback follows the **same hierarchy as the original pay**
- If someone in that chain is gone, the remaining levels still eat their share
- Rapid-lapse clawbacks can be taken from **anyone** who was in the original hierarchy
- If you don’t repay, they **withhold from future commissions**
- They also reserved the right to recover **any lawful way** they want

**Team rule:** don’t spend first-year / advance money until the policy has survived **month 3**. A 90-day lapse zeros the whole check.

## If they change or terminate the schedule

- They can **change rates** (and will tell you)
- They can **terminate the schedule** on written notice
- After termination they **keep paying existing book** while the agent is still contracted and the policies stay in force
- **90 days after termination** they can change *how* those leftover commissions are paid

Don’t assume today’s advance / residual rules last if they cut the schedule. Existing business still pays unless they rewrite the method after that 90-day window.

Not the FMO AEP MA grid. Not a dollar table. Do not invent AARP/UHC Med Supp dollar amounts. If someone asks for the rate, ask for the schedule/grid file.
