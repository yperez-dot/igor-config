# How to tell the team: Med Supp commissions (including renewals)

Two carriers. Two machines. Do not mix them. Do not quote AARP dollars for a United American case, or UA percents for AARP.

Source: United American schedule **1H98 level 04** (effective 3/1/2022) and UHC **2026 AARP Med Supp schedule** (apps signed on/after 10/1/2025, effective on/after 1/1/2026). Life/annuity is a different UA paper (**1L15**) — not Med Supp.

Tone: not friendly. Rule, then the check, then what they must not do.

## United American — percent of premium collected. No 9-month advance.

How the check is built:

1. First payment: percent of the **modal premium that came in with the app**.
2. Rest of year 1: percent of premiums collected during the **first policy year after issue**.
3. Renewals: percent of premiums collected **after year 1**.

Renewal math: they use the **premium on the issue date or the premium actually paid, whichever is lower**. If UA raises the rate later, the renewal commission does **not** go up with it.

Credit/debit card on the **first** premium: **3% less on that policy forever** (later commissions follow the first-pay method). Bank draft / check on the first premium.

The percents on the schedule are the **whole hierarchy** (this level + everyone under it). A downline’s piece comes out of this total.

Florida, age 65+ (no FL exception — STANDARD applies):

- Plans A, B, C, D, F, G, MC48: **20% year 1 and 20% every renewal year** (years 2–5, 6, and 7+).
- High-deductible F and G: **16% every year**, including renewals.
- Plans K, L, N (attained age): 24% (65–69) / 19% (70–74) / 14% (75+) for years 1–6, then **7% in year 7+**.
- First-time buyer, age 64.5–65.99, no Med Supp already in force: **28% year 1, 14% every year after** (replaces the 20% row).

Under 65 in Florida:

- Underwritten A–G: 20% every year. HD: 16%. K/L/N: 16% every year.
- Open enrollment / guaranteed issue / ESRD: **not $0 in Florida**. A–G **5%** every year; HD **4%**; K/L/N **4.2%** years 1–6 then **2.5%**. (Most other states pay $0 on that OE/GI/ESRD row.)

Other UA hits:

- Internal replacement or conversion: UA can cut or **zero** the commission.
- GI replacement of a Medicare Advantage: **renewals in years 7+ are 0%** (not Washington).
- No commission on the Part B deductible slice of premium.
- They can change the schedule on **new** business from the change date forward.

## AARP Med Supp (UHC) — fixed dollars, 9-month advance.

How the check is built:

- Paid on **collected premium**. Premium must be current. Waived premium = no commission.
- Once month-1 premium is paid: **nine months is advanced** (except internal replacements and a few company exceptions).
- Then it is earned off as premium comes in.
- Year buckets: **year 1, years 2–6, years 7–10**. After that this schedule does not list pay.
- Missing downline levels **roll up**. Paid on the next commission cycle after the app is in the enrollment system.

Florida area is the **client’s ZIP**, not the office ZIP. **Doral 33172 is Area 1.** Other FL areas pay less. Look up ZIP before quoting a number.

Florida Area 1, age 65+ (2026 schedule):

- G / Select G (also B, C, F): **$582 year 1 and years 2–6**, then **$75 years 7–10**.
- N / Select N: **$397.50** years 1–6, then **$55**.
- A, K, L: **$198.75** then **$27.50**.
- High-deductible G: **$141.50** then **$19**.

Under 65 in FL: full 65+ rates. GI outside open enrollment in FL: also full 65+ rates.

Replacements:

- AARP → another AARP plan: **usually $0**.
- Internal swap (UHIC ↔ UHICA): **90% of year-2**, year 1 equals year 2, **no advance**. Original writing agent must still be active/licensed/appointed or it can go to the replacing agent.
- Replace another carrier’s Med Supp: **year 1 = year 2** (no first-year bump).

Lapse / death / take-back:

- Lapse over 3 months and not reinstated by the agent: **no more commissions**.
- Death: paid through the **end of the month they died**.
- Unearned commissions (including advances) charged back **in full** to every level that was paid. Next check, then the next, until it is gone.
- 12-month clawback window; **rapid lapse (in force ≤ 3 months) = 100%**. Do not spend first-year / advance money until **month 3**. AARP advanced you 9 months. A 90-day lapse zeros it.

IN / OK / TN on UHICA AARP: **$0 on new apps**. Existing book still pays.

## Upfront: AARP pays more cash. UA is the rumor because of renewals.

Do not tell the team “UA pays better up front.” That is wrong.

**First check / first 9 months:** AARP. They advance **nine months** after month-1 premium. Florida Area 1 Plan G year-1 is **$582**, so the advance is in that neighborhood (about 9/12 of year 1) in one hit. UA has **no advance**. Monthly client at UA: first check is **20% of one month’s premium**. That is small money.

**Year 1 total:** AARP Area 1 Plan G is a locked **$582**. UA is **20% of year-1 collected premium**. UA only beats $582 in year 1 if that policy’s annual premium is **over ~$2,910** (~$243/month). Same math for Plan N: UA 20% vs AARP **$397.50** → UA wins year 1 only if N premium is over **~$1,988/year**. HD: UA 16% vs AARP HDG **$141.50** → UA wins year 1 only if HD premium is over **~$884/year**.

**Years 2–6:** AARP Area 1 G stays **$582**. UA stays 20% of the **issue** premium (not the hiked premium). Same breakeven: UA needs ~$243/month at issue to match.

**Years 7+:** this is where UA actually wins. UA A–G stays **20%**. AARP G drops to **$75**. If the policy is still there, UA is the better residual.

**First-time buyer at UA (64.5–65.99):** **28% year 1** — the one UA row that can look fatter in year 1. Then it drops to 14%.

Also: UA 20% is the **whole hierarchy**. Writing agent does not pocket 20% if someone sits under them. AARP $582 is the agent line on that schedule (downline rolls up if missing).

## One-breath version for the team

United American: percent of premium that actually comes in. **Every year, including renewals.** No 9-month advance. Rate hikes after issue do not grow your renewal. Card on the first premium cuts you 3% for the life of the policy.

AARP: fixed dollar by Florida ZIP area and plan. **Nine-month advance** after first premium. Years 2–6 usually stay in the same neighborhood as year 1. Years 7–10 drop hard. If it lapses in 90 days they take 100% back. Do not spend it.

Up front AARP pays more cash. UA pays better if the policy is still there in year 7. Do not tell the team UA is the better first check.
